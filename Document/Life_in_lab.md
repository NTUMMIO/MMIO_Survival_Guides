# Life in lab

> ## Latest Update: 2026-08-26



## Responsibilities

Please read this handbook when you join, and if anything is unclear, ask the PI directly. 

1. **Integrity first.** Uphold research integrity at all times. Fabrication, falsification, and plagiarism in any form are forbidden. Violations are reported immediately and handled seriously.
2. **Care for one another.** The lab has no hierarchy of respect: everyone, the PI included, treats each other with courtesy, helps one another, and collaborates. If a conflict or discomfort arises, raise it directly with the person or with the PI. Rudeness and hostility are never acceptable.
3. **Work ethic.** Be responsive and take initiative on lab-related work (teaching support, data/analysis help, and collaborations). Weekend and holiday work is not expected unless a deadline genuinely requires it.

The responsibilities below apply to everyone according to their role. Most day-to-day requests can be settled with a single question: *"Is this reasonable and fair to everyone in the lab?"*

### PI

The PI commits to:

1. Supporting your career development — for example, introducing you to researchers in the field, promoting your work in public settings, writing recommendation letters, and funding conference travel when the budget allows.
2. Encouraging you to take a better-fitting opportunity elsewhere when one exists, rather than keeping you in the lab.
3. Caring about your emotional and physical wellbeing.
4. Giving you timely feedback on research ideas, posters, slides, manuscripts, figures, and proposals.
5. Meeting and corresponding with you regularly — through lab meetings, personal meetings by request, Discord, and email — about your research or anything else you want to discuss.
6. Sharing perspectives on the lab's direction, the state of academia and industry, and how to survive and grow in a research career.

### Ph. D. Students

1. Complete your required coursework, the qualifying examination (資格考), and any assigned TA duties on schedule.
2. Take ownership of your graduation requirements — required credits, the qualifying exam, the department's publication requirement, timelines, and regulations — and keep the PI informed of where you stand. If military service (兵役) or another constraint affects your timeline, plan around it early and let the PI know.
3. Drive your research forward. Your dissertation should contain at least three representative studies or topics that together answer one significant, overarching question. Most of the work is yours to carry out independently, but remember that every labmate is a resource you can draw on.
4. Establish your own independent research direction — finding topics, executing the work, and writing and publishing papers.
5. Set and track short- and long-term career goals (academia — research or teaching — industry, science communication, and so on) and discuss them with the PI regularly so that you get the training you need.
6. Mentor and help the lab's master's and undergraduate students, whether they ask for help or the PI assigns it to you.
7. Help other lab members when they need it.

### Master's Students

1. Complete your required coursework and course-TA duties, especially in your first year when the coursework load is heaviest, then shift your focus to research in your second year.
2. Take ownership of your graduation requirements — required credits, the thesis proposal (計畫書) and the thesis defense (學位口試), any conference or publication requirement, timelines, and department regulations — and keep the PI informed. If military service (兵役) or another constraint affects your timeline, plan around it early and let the PI know.
3. Drive your thesis research forward. A master's thesis is normally a single coherent topic scoped to your two years in the program; aim for one clear, well-answered question rather than breadth. Most of the work is yours to do, but every labmate is a resource you can draw on.
4. Participate fully in lab meetings — weekly progress reports and journal club (see *Life in lab → Meetings*).
5. Take on a rotating lab role during your time here (Lab TA, IT Manager, Lab Contact Person and Safety Monitor, or Course TA — see *Roles*), keep it running smoothly, and help mentor whoever takes it over from you.
6. Help other lab members when they need it.
7. Think about your career direction — industry, a PhD, or something else — and discuss it with the PI so that your training matches your goals.




## Roles

### Lab TA

Normally assigned to a 2nd year master’s student. 
Responsibilities include:
1.	Being the TA of the Undergraduate Research course and maintaining its course materials
2.	Being the contact person for research-related discussions and resources

### IT Manager

Normally assigned to a 2nd year master’s student. 
Responsibilities include:
1.	Handling server-related incidents and recording them on the GitHub log
2.	Contacting the server vendor for maintenance assistance
3.	Assisting with hardware purchases
4.	Making sure hardware are powered down before scheduled outages and storms and powered up after (with Safety Monitor)
5.	Keeping a log of hardware specs, monitor power usage, manage router/IP/remote login
6.	Sudo user; maintaining user accounts
7.	Identifying and mentoring the next IT manager

### Lab Contact Person and Safety Monitor

Normally assigned to a master’s student for their entire tenure in the program. 
Responsibilities include:
1.	Forwarding (e.g. posting to the lab Discord server) safety-related notices (e.g. power outages) from the department office to the lab
2.	Maintaining list of alumni
3.	Assisting with filling out forms requested by the department office
4.	Routine safety inspections
5.	Making sure hardware are powered down before scheduled outages and storms and powered up after (with IT Manager)

## Life in lab

### Meetings

We normally have weekly lab meetings on Wednesday afternoons at 1PM-2:20PM in the fall semester, Thursday afternoons starting 1PM in the spring semester, in YL430. The start time may fluctuate during summer and winter breaks but will be announced beforehand by the PI. Meetings are divided into four parts: 1) Progress report: every student records their progress on the Google sheet and reports what they’ve done over the past week. 2) Journal club: each semester every graduate student will share a paper (ideally published within the past 5 years) they’ve read to the lab. Normally one student reports per week; you will sign up in the Lab Schedules Google sheet. In the fall semester, we will have a technical theme for papers and in the spring semester we will have a imaging/clinical theme for papers (to be merged with the Emission Tomography course if the course is offered). 3) Deep dive into results: if needed by the student. 4) General lab-related items for discussion: if needed.

You may bring food with you during lab meeting but take out any waste with you after.

Personal meetings are by request.

### Lab space and hardware

For full-time master’s and PhD students, you will receive a cubicle as your personal space in the lab. A desktop computer will be provided for your lab/course work, and will have a GPU installed for local training purposes. For undergraduate students and other personnel, lab space and computers will be granted on a case-by-case basis and depending on availability. USB drives will also be provided upon request; please return upon graduation. Please respect the space and property of your labmates.

### Trash

We do not assign daily monitors to take out the trash in lab, so everyone is responsible for taking out their own trash, especially perishable (food) waste! We do not want actual bugs in our lab either!

### Fridge

The fridge is personal property of the PI. Feel free to use the fridge for temporary food/drink storage. We assign monthly monitors to clean the fridge at the end of the month. Everything that is in the fridge by the last day of the month (default cleaning time: 17:00) will be thrown away. The cleaning time, if changed, will be announced by the monthly monitor at least 24 hours before the scheduled cleaning time on Discord.
